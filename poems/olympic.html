Olympic

by Michael Penta · Version 2

← All writingRead the alternate draft (Version 1)

Yesterday, a scrim of trees staged along the roadway masked the massacre of their cousins.

You gasped. I turned to your face weighted with grief. Your mourning tightened and twisted in my stomach.

This morning, mist screens a mountain lake fringed with fir and hemlock. My groan disturbs the drumming of rain on the taut nylon tent.

"Dude. We're in a rainforest. Remember?"

Beneath moss-cloaked branches we walk as mammals among outstretched ferns.

For days, you, me, and verdancy.

At the coast, gray rock, sea stacks crowned with spruce, Pacific tides washing up trunks of driftwood. We climb the stark white, sun-dried bodies of our fallen friends.
