# Olympic

*by Michael Penta*

The morning mist  
veils a mountain lake  
fringed with fir and hemlock.  
My groan  
disturbs the drumming of rain  
on the taut nylon tent.  
“Rainforest, remember?”

Yesterday,  
a scrim of trees staged along the roadway  
masked the massacre of their cousins.  
Your gasp weighted with grief.  
I turn to your face,  
and your mourning  
tightened and twisted in my stomach.

Beneath moss-cloaked branches  
we walk as mammals  
among outstretched ferns.

For days,  
you, me,  
and verdancy.

At the coast,  
gray rock,  
sea stacks crowned with spruce,  
Pacific tides  
washing up trunks of driftwood.  
We climb the stark white, sun-dried bodies  
of our fallen friends
